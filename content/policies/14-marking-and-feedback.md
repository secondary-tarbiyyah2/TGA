## 1. Purpose

Feedback is one of the most powerful tools a teacher has. This policy sets out how feedback is given in an online school so that it is timely, specific, acted on by pupils, and sustainable for teachers.

## 2. Principles

- Feedback should tell pupils what they did well and exactly what to do next.
- Pupils must act on feedback, and time is given for them to do so.
- Most feedback happens in the lesson, live.
- Written marking is used where it has the greatest impact, not as a record for adults.
- Workload matters: whole-class feedback replaces much individual written marking.

## 3. Forms of Feedback

| Form | How it works online |
| --- | --- |
| Live verbal feedback | During lessons, through questioning, polls and reviewing work shared on screen or in the Student Portal |
| Whole-class feedback | After reviewing a set of work, the teacher shares common strengths, misconceptions and next steps at the start of the next lesson; pupils then improve their work |
| Individual written feedback | Comments on key pieces of work in the Student Portal, using rubrics where appropriate |
| Self and peer assessment | Pupils check work against success criteria; peer feedback is structured and supervised |
| Automatic feedback | Quizzes in the Student Portal give instant results and explanations for knowledge checks |

## 4. Expectations

| Area | Expectation |
| --- | --- |
| Key assessed pieces | Individual written feedback with at least one clear next step, returned within ten school days |
| Homework | Acknowledged and reviewed through whole-class feedback or quiz results; not every piece receives individual comments |
| Pupil response | Pupils complete "improvement time" tasks in response to feedback, visible in the Student Portal or assignment |
| Literacy | Teachers correct key spelling, punctuation and grammar errors in extended writing |
| Qur'an and Arabic | Mainly live, individual feedback on recitation and pronunciation |

In English, Mathematics and Science, each pupil receives individual written feedback on at least one key piece of work every two weeks; in other subjects, at least once every half term.

## 5. Monitoring and Review

Leaders check the quality and impact of feedback through work scrutiny through the Student Portal and pupil voice. The policy is reviewed annually, with attention to teacher workload.
