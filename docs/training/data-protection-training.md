## 1. Why this matters here

The personal data of our pupils, families and staff is an amanah, a trust. In an online
school almost everything is personal data: the register, a lesson recording, a message to a
parent, a pupil's homework, a screenshot of a class list. Most breaches are not hackers;
they are an email to the wrong person, a list downloaded to a laptop, or a screen shared at
the wrong moment.

The law is the **UK GDPR** and the **Data Protection Act 2018**. The Information
Commissioner's Office (ICO) can fine and publish its findings. More importantly, families
trust us with their children.

## 2. The six principles, in plain words

Personal data must be:

1. **Lawful, fair and open:** we only use it in ways families have been told about (the
   privacy notice on the website).
2. **For a clear purpose:** collected for education and running the school, not reused for
   something else.
3. **The minimum needed:** ask only for what you need; do not copy more than you need.
4. **Accurate:** correct it when you know it is wrong.
5. **Kept no longer than needed:** follow the retention schedule (policy 28, section 12).
6. **Secure:** protected from loss, theft and the wrong eyes.

And we must be able to **show** we follow them (accountability): records, logs and training.

## 3. Special category data

Some data needs extra care: **health and medical information, SEND, religious belief,
ethnicity, and biometric data**. In a Muslim school, religion is part of everyday life, but
it is still special category data when it is recorded about a named person. Safeguarding
records are not a special category in law, but treat them with the same care: only the DSL
and authorised staff see them.

## 4. Every day: what good looks like

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Use only Academy accounts (portal, Microsoft 365) for anything about pupils or families | Use personal email, WhatsApp, personal cloud storage or a personal phone's photos |
| Open only the records you need for your job | Look up a pupil or family out of curiosity: every record opened is in the audit log |
| Lock your screen (Windows + L, or Ctrl + Cmd + Q on a Mac) whenever you step away | Leave the portal open on a shared family computer |
| Use multi-factor authentication and a password you use nowhere else | Share your login, even with a colleague |
| Close other tabs and notifications before sharing your screen in a lesson | Share a screen that shows a class list, a medical note or a message |
| Put families in **Bcc** in group emails, or use portal Messages | Put a list of parents' emails in To or Cc |
| Check the recipient before pressing Send | Reply-all to a thread with families in it |
| Keep data in the portal; print only when you must, then shred | Download pupil lists or reports to your own device or USB stick |
| Use only AI tools the Academy has approved, without pupils' personal data | Paste a pupil's name, report, work or medical details into an unapproved AI tool |
| Tell the DPO straight away if something goes wrong | Try to fix a breach quietly or wait to see if anyone notices |

## 5. Working from home or abroad

Many of our staff work from home, and some from other countries.

- Work in a place where others cannot see your screen or hear calls about pupils.
- Keep your device's operating system and browser up to date, with a screen lock.
- Data stays in Academy systems (Supabase, London; Microsoft 365). Do not move it to a
  service in another country.
- If you are going to work from a different country, tell the Headteacher first: the
  Academy may need a transfer risk assessment (policy 28, section 8).

## 6. Sharing information

- **Safeguarding comes first.** Data protection is never a reason not to share information
  needed to keep a child safe. If you are worried about a child, report to the DSL straight
  away through Report a concern; the DSL decides what to share and with whom.
- **Parents** can see their own child's information, not anyone else's. Be careful with
  separated parents: check the pupil record for who holds parental responsibility and any
  court orders, and ask the office if unsure.
- **Other organisations** (another school, a doctor, a local authority) only through the
  office or the DSL, never by forwarding from your own inbox.
- **Photos and video for publicity** need specific written consent; check the pupil record.

## 7. Requests from individuals

Anyone can ask for the information we hold about them (a **subject access request**), or
ask us to correct or delete it. A request does not have to use the right words or be in
writing: "can I see everything you have on my son?" in a message counts.

- Pass it to the DPO **the same day**. We have one calendar month to answer.
- Do not answer it yourself, and do not delete anything once a request has been made.
- Remember that anything you write about a pupil, including in messages and comments, may be
  seen by the family. Write factually and kindly.

## 8. Breaches

A breach is any loss, wrong disclosure, change or destruction of personal data, by accident
or on purpose. Examples: an email to the wrong parent; a lost laptop or phone with Academy
email; someone joining a lesson who should not be there; a class list shared on screen; a
compromised password.

1. **Report it to the DPO immediately**, and in any case within 24 hours (policy 02). For a
   lost device or compromised account, tell the Digital Lead too, so access can be cut off.
2. Do what you safely can to contain it (ask the wrong recipient to delete the email and
   confirm; change your password).
3. The DPO decides whether to report it to the ICO, which must happen within **72 hours** of
   the Academy becoming aware, and whether families must be told.
4. Every breach is recorded, even small ones, so we learn from them. Nobody is punished for
   reporting a mistake quickly.

## 9. Recordings, retention and deletion

- Lesson recordings are kept for safeguarding and catch-up for one academic year, then
  deleted (policy 04 and policy 28, section 12). Never download, share or re-upload them.
- Delete your own working copies (exports, drafts, screenshots) when the job is done.
- Records are deleted by the Digital Lead on the schedule; do not delete official records
  yourself.

## 10. Audit logs

The portal records who opened and changed what. The Digital Lead reviews the audit logs every
month and whenever a concern is raised (policy 02, section 4). This protects pupils and it
protects you: it shows you only opened what your job needed.

## Quiz

Ten questions. Pass mark 8. Answers and the reason are at the end.

1. A parent emails asking for "everything the school has written about my daughter this year".
   What do you do?
   - a) Reply with her reports and your notes
   - b) Pass it to the DPO the same day
   - c) Ask the parent to fill in a formal form before anything else happens
   - d) Ignore it unless they use the words "subject access request"

2. Which of these is special category data?
   - a) A pupil's year group
   - b) A pupil's medical condition
   - c) A parent's email address
   - d) A pupil's homework mark

3. You need to send a letter to all Year 8 parents by email. How?
   - a) All addresses in the To line
   - b) All addresses in Cc
   - c) All addresses in Bcc, or through portal Messages
   - d) Forward it from your personal email so it arrives faster

4. You realise you sent a pupil's report to the wrong parent ten minutes ago. What first?
   - a) Wait to see if the parent mentions it
   - b) Tell the DPO immediately and ask the recipient to delete it
   - c) Send the right report and say nothing
   - d) Delete it from your Sent folder

5. How long does the Academy have to tell the ICO about a reportable breach?
   - a) 24 hours
   - b) 72 hours from becoming aware
   - c) One month
   - d) There is no time limit

6. A pupil tells you something that makes you worried about their safety, but asks you to
   keep it secret "because of data protection". What do you do?
   - a) Keep it secret
   - b) Report it to the DSL straight away; data protection never stops sharing to keep a child safe
   - c) Ask the parents first
   - d) Write it in an email to the whole staff

7. You want to use an AI tool to help write report comments. What is allowed?
   - a) Any AI tool, with the pupil's full name and grades
   - b) Only Academy-approved tools, without pupils' personal data unless the tool is approved for it
   - c) A free online tool, as long as you delete the chat afterwards
   - d) None, ever

8. You are about to share your screen in a live lesson. What should you check?
   - a) Nothing, pupils won't notice
   - b) That no class lists, messages, medical notes or other tabs are visible
   - c) Only that your camera is on
   - d) That the lesson is not recorded

9. Out of interest, you look up a pupil you don't teach in the portal. Why is this a problem?
   - a) It isn't: all staff can see all pupils
   - b) It breaks the "minimum needed" principle, and the audit log records it
   - c) It slows the portal down
   - d) Only because the pupil might find out

10. Where should you keep a list of your class's names and contact details?
    - a) In a spreadsheet on your own laptop
    - b) In a WhatsApp note
    - c) In the portal, where it already is
    - d) Printed on your desk at home

### Answers

1. **b.** It is a subject access request, whatever words are used. The DPO answers it within
   one month.
2. **b.** Health is special category data. The others are personal data, but not special category.
3. **c.** Bcc (or portal Messages) keeps families' addresses private from each other.
4. **b.** Report immediately; the 72-hour clock may already be running.
5. **b.** 72 hours from the Academy becoming aware. (Staff report to the DPO within 24 hours.)
6. **b.** Never promise secrecy; safeguarding comes first.
7. **b.** Only approved tools; pupils' personal data never goes into an unapproved tool.
8. **b.** Screen sharing is one of the commonest ways data leaks in an online school.
9. **b.** Access only what your job needs; every record opened is logged.
10. **c.** Keep data in Academy systems; never copy it to personal devices or apps.
