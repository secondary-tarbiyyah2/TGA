# Virtual Guest Speaker and External Content Risk Assessment

**Tarbiyyah Global Academy.** The form named in the Safeguarding: Extremism and
Radicalisation Policy (policy 05, section 5) and the Risk Assessment Policy
(policy 11, section 4). Owner's request, 11 November 2026 (Tafweed 3, T54).

Use one form for each guest speaker, webinar, visiting organisation or external
resource (video, website, workbook, app or lesson pack written outside the
Academy) used with pupils. The member of staff organising it fills in parts 1 to
5; the Designated Safeguarding Lead (DSL), or the Headteacher if the DSL is away,
completes part 6. Nothing goes ahead, and no joining link is shared, until part 6
says "Approved". Keep the completed form with the DSL, in line with the Data
Protection Policy.

Risk ratings use the Risk Assessment Policy's scale: **likelihood 1 to 5 ×
impact 1 to 5**. 1 to 6 is low (normal routines), 8 to 12 is medium (specific
controls; review before the session), 15 to 25 is high (not approved unless the
controls bring it down, and the Headteacher is told).

## Part 4. Controls for a live session

Tick each control that will be in place. Anything not ticked needs a reason in "Notes".

- [ ] A member of Academy staff hosts the meeting from an Academy account and stays for the whole session.
- [ ] The host can mute, remove or end the session at any time, and knows how to.
- [ ] The guest joins as an external guest through the lobby; pupils cannot share their screens.
- [ ] The guest shares their screen only with the host's approval.
- [ ] Pupils cannot message the guest privately; questions go through the lesson chat or the host.
- [ ] No contact details are exchanged between the guest and pupils, during or after the session.
- [ ] The session is recorded in line with the Recording of Live Lessons and Use of Images Policy.
- [ ] Pupils are told beforehand what the session is about and how to raise a worry afterwards.
- [ ] The host has the DSL's contact details for the session time.

## Reporting and referral, wherever the pupil lives

If anything in the session, or anything a pupil says afterwards, raises a concern:

1. **Immediate danger:** call the emergency services where the pupil is (999 in
   the UK; the local number elsewhere, listed in the DSL's Emergency Contact
   Register), then tell the DSL.
2. **Otherwise:** report to the DSL the same day through Report a concern in the
   portal. Do not investigate or question the pupil yourself.
3. **The DSL decides on referral:**
   - **pupils living in the UK:** children's services at the local authority
     where the pupil lives (in Scotland, the council's social work service; in
     Northern Ireland, the Health and Social Care Trust), and the police where
     there is a risk of harm;
   - **pupils living outside the UK:** the child protection or social services
     and the police in the country where the pupil lives, using the Emergency
     Contact Register. Where the right service is unclear, the DSL seeks advice
     from the London Borough of Hounslow's children's services, and for British
     nationals may contact the Foreign, Commonwealth and Development Office and
     the local authority where the family last lived in the UK;
   - **concerns about the speaker:** the DSL tells the speaker's organisation
     where appropriate, records the decision not to invite them again, and, if
     the speaker may pose a risk to children, takes advice from the local
     authority.
4. The DSL records every decision and the reasons, including a decision not to
   refer.

The DfE counter-extremism helpline (020 7340 7264,
counter.extremism@education.gov.uk) is available for non-emergency advice, as
in policy 05.
